Submission volume is up in 2026.

Brokers want answers faster and carriers are asking harder questions about how risks get picked. All of that pressure hits intake and quoting first, and our new whitepaper covers what it costs.

Submissions turn up in whatever shape the broker sends them so one email carries an ACORD, an SOV, a loss run, a prior policy and a few scans while the next broker sends the same account another way. Missing ACORD fields stop the quote and an SOV that has not been cleaned up can pull property and package quotes 20% or more off. The whitepaper maps six of these issues against what each one does to the MGA.

Slow answers cost money as well because a quote that takes two to five days often lands after the broker has bound elsewhere. Deciding whether a submission is worth quoting matters as much as quoting fast, and the whitepaper breaks down the five triage calls behind that decision.

Rework is harder to spot because a file looks processed and moves on until the cost comes back as a broker question, a pricing change, a carrier referral and a reissued quote. Carrier partners watch for that pattern and [Vertafore's 2026](https://www.vertafore.com/resources/blog/2026-mga-outlook-scaling-smarter-demanding-market) outlook names underwriting consistency, loss ratio stability and portfolio discipline as what capacity providers now expect.

The whitepaper also covers the systems sprawl that spreads one submission across seven tools, the specialty lines that carry usable loss history on only 31% of submissions against 73% in traditional lines and the reason hiring no longer clears an intake backlog.

We ranked all ten because no MGA fixes them all at once, and number one is the problem the other nine flow from. The ranking sits on page 11, and the intake layer that addresses it is on the page after.
