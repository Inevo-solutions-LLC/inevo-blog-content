## Where to start

The highest-leverage automation targets share three traits: high volume, low judgment, and clear inputs. In commercial intake that is usually:

1. Pulling data out of submission emails and ACORDs.
2. Clearance and conflict checks against existing policies.
3. Producing a structured submission record for the underwriting workbench.

## Where to stop

Anything that requires negotiation, broker relationships, or appetite-edge judgment is not automation territory. Those are the moments underwriters earn their pay; automation should buy them time, not replace them.

## Measuring ROI

Track three numbers every month:

- **Time-to-quote** — wall-clock from submission received to first quote issued.
- **Touch count** — how many people handle a submission before it binds.
- **Hit ratio** — quoted vs bound. If automation increases quote volume but tanks hit ratio, you are quoting the wrong business faster.

## The integration tax

The hardest part of insurance automation is rarely the AI — it is wiring policy admin systems, document stores, and broker portals together. Budget for it.
